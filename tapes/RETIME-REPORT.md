# Tape re-timing report (T1)

Generated by `tapes/retime_tapes.py`. Every tape with a matching
`say/<stem>.mp3` was re-timed: the section shape comes from its
`# M:SS-M:SS` headers, the scale from the mp3's ffprobe duration.

## These numbers are a static model, and they have NOT been validated by rendering

**Nothing in this report was rendered.** Every "predicted" duration below is the
modelled sum of a tape's `Sleep` directives plus its typing cost. That is not what
`vhs` actually produces.

E5 worker-2 measured the gap on this box. Rendering `story-019-stacked-panes`
against a static `Sleep` budget of **20.550s** produced an mp4 whose `ffprobe`
duration was **2.000s** -- a drift of **0.097** of the budget, at load 63-131. The
same tape rendered on 2026-05-29 came out at 182 frames = 7.28s, a drift of
**0.354**. The mechanism: every output is 25fps and its duration is
`nb_frames / 25`, so real output length tracks vhs's frame-capture throughput, not
the `Sleep` directives this tool rewrites. **The drift is load-dependent and
unbounded, and no constant correction factor exists** -- which is why this tool
does not apply one.

Therefore, stated plainly:

- These tapes are **not** "correctly timed" now. They are re-timed against a model.
- **Re-timing alone will not make the 111 masters pass QC.** A render on a quiet
  box plus per-file `ffprobe` verification against the narration is also required.
- The ~3.7x narration overrun recorded in `E5-video.md` section 1 is **not**
  demonstrated fixed by this report. Nothing here re-rendered or re-measured a
  master.

What this work does establish is narrower and still necessary: the tapes genuinely
were mis-timed against their narration, their storyboard shape is now scaled to a
real measured input (`ffprobe` on `say/*.mp3`), and the three tapes that cannot be
re-timed at all are identified below.

The cause is the same in all three: **the tape's own typing takes longer than
the whole narration**. Each of these stories builds a skill file by typing
dozens of `echo '...' >> .rysh/humanoids/support.md` lines at 75ms/char, so one
section alone costs 40-62s against a 47-48s narration. A section's floor is its
typing cost plus a 0.10s minimum `Sleep` per directive, and re-timing cannot go
below that floor without deleting typing -- which would change what the video
shows. **These are not re-time cases.** Each needs one of: a `Type@` speed
override for the file-authoring block, replacing the typed heredoc with a
`cat` of a prepared file, or a longer narration. That is a separate decision,
not something this tool should make silently.

Note for whoever picks that up: these are exactly the three tutorials
`E5-video.md` section 5 flags as carrying an adapter claim that must be checked
against `new_roadmap/designs/024-investor-claims.md` before they go out. Do not
re-cut them on timing grounds alone.
