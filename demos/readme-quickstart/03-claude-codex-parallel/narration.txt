# Demo 3 — narration. One cue per `# @section N` in demo.tape.

[1]
Rysh doesn't care which agent you run, or how many. New stack two, and this lane
now holds three panes in one group. Stacked, Zellij style: one open, the others
collapsed to a title bar with their number on it.

[2]
Now fill them. Control S and a digit jumps straight to a pane, so pane one gets
Claude Code. Pane two gets Codex instead — a different vendor, a different
process, in the same session. Pane three gets a second Claude.

[3]
Each one gets its own instruction, typed straight into its pane. There is no
orchestrator here and no shared queue — every pane is just an agent, being told
what to do by the person sitting in front of it.

[4]
And now flip through the stack. All three are working at once. You see one at a
time because they're stacked, but nothing is waiting its turn — three agent
processes, each with its own shell and its own conversation, running side by
side underneath.

[5]
Three files, written by three agents, in parallel, in one session. Everything in
this video came out of a single terminal window and a binary you install with
one command.
